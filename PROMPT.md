# The original prompt & generation settings

Everything on this page is what actually produced the PHONEX ULTRA footage —
copied verbatim from the working pipeline, with nothing server-side attached.
The resulting film itself is committed at [`media/film.mp4`](media/film.mp4),
so every step below can be reproduced or skipped.

## 1. The video prompt (verbatim)

The film is generated by an **image-to-video** model. The product photo is sent
as the clip's *first frame*, and this prompt describes the rest. Replace
`{subject}` with your product name (optionally with its category in
parentheses, e.g. `PHONEX ULTRA (smartphone)`):

```text
Cinematic exploded-view product film of the {subject} from the reference photo,
in a dark premium studio: near-black background, soft rim lighting, subtle
floor reflection. The product floats in the center of frame, slowly tumbling.
Then it cleanly separates into its internal layers and components — housing,
frame, boards, modules — each part drifting apart along one axis into an evenly
spaced exploded view, every component crisp and suspended in space. It holds
the fully exploded arrangement for a beat, then every part glides back and
reassembles precisely into the original product. Single continuous shot, camera
slowly orbiting the product. Photorealistic, high detail, consistent
perspective and lighting throughout. No text overlays, no captions, no logos
other than on the product itself, no hands, no people, no cuts.
```

**Why the prohibitions at the end matter:** image-to-video models love to
invent hands presenting the product, captions naming it, and hard cuts. Each of
those ruins a scroll-scrubbed page, because the frame sequence must read as
**one continuous transformation**.

## 2. Generation parameters

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Model class | Veo 3.1 (fast) — Seedance / Sora 2-class models also work | image-to-video with first-frame control |
| First frame | the uploaded product photo (sent inline as a data URL) | the film starts on *your* product, not the model's idea of it |
| Duration | 10 seconds (8 s also works) | enough for tumble → explode → hold → reassemble; the committed film is 10 s |
| Resolution | 720p | the site scrubs frames full-bleed at 1280px wide — more is wasted bytes |
| Aspect ratio | 16:9 | landscape hero |
| Audio | off | frames are extracted; sound would never be heard |

Bring your own access to any Veo / Seedance / Sora-class **image-to-video**
service — a web UI or an API, whichever you have. By design this repo includes
no vendor account, endpoint, or key details; the prompt and the settings above
are everything you need to reproduce the film.

## 3. From film to scroll data: the frame extraction

The page never plays the mp4. It paints JPEG frames onto a `<canvas>` as you
scroll (seeking a `<video>` is neither instant nor frame-accurate across
browsers). The pipeline extracts **up to 120 evenly spaced frames**:

```bash
# 1) read the clip's real duration
ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 \
  -show_entries stream=avg_frame_rate -show_entries format=duration \
  -of json film.mp4

# 2) extract ≤120 evenly spaced 1280px-wide JPEGs (replace 10 with your duration in seconds)
ffmpeg -y -i film.mp4 -vf "fps=120/10,scale=1280:-2" -q:v 4 \
  -frames:v 120 -start_number 0 site/assets/frames/frame_%03d.jpg

# 3) the poster is the first frame (which IS the original product photo)
cp site/assets/frames/frame_000.jpg site/assets/img/poster.jpg
```

Notes from the working pipeline:

- If the clip has **fewer** source frames than 120, drop the `fps=` filter and
  take them all — resampling up just writes duplicates.
- `scale=1280:-2` keeps aspect and stays codec-legal (even height); `-q:v 4` is
  visually clean for photographic content at a fraction of the bytes of `-q:v 2`.
- `-frames:v 120` is a hard ceiling — fps-filter rounding can emit one extra
  frame.
- The site's JS hardcodes `FRAME_COUNT = 120` (`site/main.js`); if your clip
  yields fewer frames, set it to the number that actually landed on disk.

## 4. The still images

The five stills in `site/assets/img/` (`poster`, `phone-assembled`,
`phone-exploded-labeled`, `feature-screen`, `feature-layers`) are frames/crops
from the same AI-generated film — pick the strongest moments of your own film
for these. (`phone-assembled.jpg` is a spare: committed for reuse, referenced
by nothing on the page.)
